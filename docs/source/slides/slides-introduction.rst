Importance
----------

A slide deck is a visual document. (A slide 'deck', as in a 'deck' of cards, is a collection of slides.) In the same way that humans tend to organize information into documents, we tend to organize key points and images into slides. The information in slides tends to be more brief and more visual – with charts and images – than a standard document. Slides are often shared and viewed as standalone materials, like a ‘fun’ version of a document, or a more 'serious' version of a graphic design project.
However, slides are most powerful when used as supporting material for a live presentation. We humans love giving presentations to other humans, whether live and recorded, and visual information perfectly complements the verbal information. Slides with just a single word or a single picture can make a speech much more impactful. Humans will always want to give presentations to others, so the need for a slide deck of some kind will never go away.
Professional work regularly involves making slides and giving presentations. Having the skills to efficiently create an elegant, effective slide deck is well worth our time.

Warm-up questions
-----------------

* When you look at slide decks, do you pay to the design of the slides? (Do you notice when the slides are well designed? Are you annoyed when the slides are poorly designed?)
* How do you pick the colours, images and templates that you use for your slides?
* When you present, do you feel that your audience should be focusing on you or focusing on your slides?
